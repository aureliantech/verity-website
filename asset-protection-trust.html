What it does

What an Asset Protection Trust Actually Does

An asset protection trust moves your property out of your direct ownership and into an irrevocable trust — managed by an independent trustee — protecting your family's wealth from future creditors.

Property placed in the trust is generally out of reach of litigation, lawsuits, and creditor claims filed after it's properly funded.

Someone other than you manages the trust day-to-day, which is part of what makes the protection hold up in court.

Depending on how it's structured, you can still benefit from the trust's assets, even though you no longer own them outright.

Complements an LLC or corporation by shielding your personal assets too, not just your business ones.

Works alongside your will and other trusts, so nothing is left exposed or duplicated.

What's left in the trust can be directed to your beneficiaries after your death, similar to other trusts.

What it doesn't do

What an Asset Protection Trust Doesn't Do

Said plainly, so you know what else to plan for.

Only future creditors and lawsuits are covered. A court can unwind a transfer made to dodge a claim you already knew about.

It's irrevocable — unlike a living trust, you generally can't undo it once it's funded.

It limits what a judgment can reach — it doesn't stop you from being sued in the first place. Liability insurance still matters.

Common Mistakes We Watch For
